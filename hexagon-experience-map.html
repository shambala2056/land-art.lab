Ambient Forest

TOOL 01

Every tree generates electrical signals. As leaf stomata open, sap is drawn upward through the trunk, and the plant responds to stress, its cell membrane potential shifts — this is bioelectric potential. Sensors across the site measure this alongside chlorophyll fluorescence and sap flow. Converting these three signals into sound lets the forest compose its own music. Moving the timeline above changes the music in real time.

Soil Indicator

TOOL 03

Glazed ceramic tiles embedded in the site's soil. The glaze is mixed with an anthocyanin-based indicator pigment that shifts colour with the soil solution's pH. Gobi soil is alkaline — pale olive at first. As organic matter accumulates, pH falls and the tiles shift from green toward blue-violet. The colour is itself the measurement: a passerby sees the soil recovering, no lab required.

Water-Minimal Planting Model

TOOL 04

Given a water budget, an optimisation algorithm tests thousands of species combinations to find the best mix within that constraint. It balances three goals at once: canopy cover, species diversity, and vertical structure. The resulting mix is rendered below as a planting diagram — the less water available, the sparser the branches and the deeper the roots.
