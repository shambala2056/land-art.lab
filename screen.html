HEXAGON

The third work, and the 2026–2032 programme. Fifty-four hexagonal cells set out by GPS on a fenced three-hectare site, 4,571 m² of it under planting. Cells run from 12 m² to 400 m², each with a fixed position, an identifier and a named steward. The build order is dependency-driven — water and power, enclosure, buildings, then planting in hardiness order — because on this ground the sequence, not the species list, decides survival.

(04) · 2026 · with Jack's Coffee

JACK'S COFFEE

The first work commissioned by a company in its own mark. A quarter of a hectare at Erdene sum, Dornogovi, planted with 10,000 Siberian elm in the form of the Jack's Coffee symbol — a mark 63 by 73 metres, read by drone survey rather than from orbit at this scale, on ground that carried nothing before it. The same method as the three works before it: water and enclosure first, then planting, then ten years of maintenance and irrigation against a survival target counted twice a year.

At COP17. Jack's Coffee is serving at the conference site in Ulaanbaatar from 17 to 28 August, where one cup carries one tree: every specially marked coffee plants an elm in the degraded Gobi rangeland at Erdene. A delegate can taste the collaboration and read the coordinates of what it plants on the same afternoon.

How a cup becomes a tree. Three COP17 editions are served — a sea-buckthorn matcha latte, an anise espresso and a blueberry ube latte — at the conference booth, at any Jack's Coffee branch in Ulaanbaatar, or online. Each purchase registers one elm in the buyer's own name at Erdene, maintained and irrigated on the same terms as the rest of the site, and reported to them by email once a year. A named tree with a fixed location is a restoration unit, not a gesture — which is the whole point of doing it this way.
