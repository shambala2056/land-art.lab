## 1 · Who we are

Land-art Space is a programme of Shambala Carbon Offsets LLC, working on
desertified ground in Dornogovi aimag, Erdene sum, in the Mongolian Gobi.

We work as three arms, and the relationship between them is the whole design:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Land Art Hub** | The physical site | Artworks, nursery, irrigation. Revenue is generated here and local jobs are created here. |
| **Land Art Lab** | The evidence | Species trials, geometry measurements, biodiversity monitoring, MRV methodology. Turns the Hub's work into data. |
| **Land Art Community** | The people | Nature-based education and vocational training for local children, connected directly to jobs at Hub and Lab. |

Around 60 organisations already stand behind the work, among them Rio Tinto, TDB,
M Bank, Nomin Holding, Yves Rocher, Timberland and the Embassy of Italy in
Ulaanbaatar. Delivery partners include Oim Green Studio LLC, Ungu Nem NGO and
Plastic Center LLC.

## 2 · What we do

We plant trees in deliberate geometry, on ground where nothing was growing, and
then prove what happened.

**Two works are already in the ground.**

| Work | Planted | Area | Trees | Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BE HUMAN** | June 2021 | 30,000 m² | 21,857 | A 300 × 100 m band with a message written inside it in trees — legible from satellite |
| **ART N TECH** | July 2023 | 38,970 m² | 36,000 | A 300 m equilateral triangle with an open centre, a ring road and three spurs, paired with a 10 kW solar system |

**Every site follows the same sixteen steps**, in four stages: survey and design
(hydrology, soil, working drawings, composting); infrastructure (deep wells,
irrigation tanks, renewable power, cultivation, fencing); planting (pits, sapling
transport and inoculation, drip connection); and monitoring (control systems, pest
management, satellite tracking). The conditions are built before anything is
planted — that is what decides survival.

**And it is independently measured.** Since 2024 the site has been monitored via
NASA MODIS, Sentinel-5P and Sentinel-2, with the data processed independently by
Martensite Analytica against an unrestored control site alongside it:

| Index | Project site | Control | |
|---|---|---|---|
| NDVI — vegetation health | **0.1834** | 0.1388 | +32% |
| EVI — vegetation density | **0.1318** | 0.1055 | +25% |
| FAPAR — light absorbed | **0.2313** | 0.1751 | +32% |
| Net carbon gain, annual average | **1,898 kg** | — | vs. control |

That control site is the argument. It is the same ground, the same rainfall, the
same year — the only difference is that we planted one of them.

## 3 · Why we do it

**The physical reason.** The site gets about 100 mm of rain a year and evaporation
runs several times higher. Wind moves the open sand freely across bare gravel.
But trees slow the wind, and drifting sand settles at their base, building a mound
called a *nebkha* that holds moisture and improves the soil. The tree holds the
sand and the sand feeds the tree. Butterflies and hares come back. That loop is
the mechanism we are buying with every cell.

**The human reason, which matters more.** Desertification strips herder households
of their livelihood. Families leave the land for the city, and their children grow
up displaced from a place they no longer have the skills to return to. Those
children are Community's target group.

So the programme closes a loop:

1. Desertification takes the pasture, and households lose their living.
2. Hub creates land art on the degraded ground, plants trees, generates jobs.
3. A share of Hub revenue funds Community, which educates children from those households.
4. Lab verifies the methods through trials and monitoring.
5. Graduates become foresters, drone operators, MRV technicians and guides — and stay in their home region.
6. Restored land reduces desertification in turn, which is where we came in.

A restored hectare that produces employment is worth more than a restored hectare
that produces only trees.

## 4 · What we are going to do

**HEXAGON, 2026 → 2032.** One hundred hexagonal cells across a 30,000 m² site:
93 woodland cells and 7 infrastructure cells. Cells run from 25 m² to 400 m², and
each has its own code and its own steward. 10,875 m² planted, 28,425 trees.

**The seven infrastructure cells are built first**, because the conditions come
before the planting: a 100 kW solar field, water harvesting tanks, a recycling
point, a research station, a bird refuge, a pollinator meadow and the community
ground.

**What the ground does, on a known schedule:**

| Years | Stage | |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Start** | Seedlings go into the hexagonal cell structure, drip irrigation feeds each cell separately. The most fragile stage — roots have not reached depth, so irrigation never stops. Survival is counted in full twice a year and losses are replanted. |
| 3–7 | **Nebkha effect** | Young trees slow the wind, sand settles, micro-dunes form. Soil builds and wild bees return. A sapling that survives three summers usually stays alive. |
| 8–15 | **Moisture retention** | Roots deepen and reach groundwater. Soil moisture stabilises; insects and rodents nest. |
| 15+ | **Ecosystem network** | The canopy closes, birds nest, and pollination, decomposition and nutrient cycling become self-sustaining. |

**What the programme is committed to producing:**

- **10,000 m²** restored — The Hive artwork
- **120** local children in training each year
- **20** permanent local jobs
- **1** Land Art Hub building — the project core

**And the site will speak for itself.** Sensors across the planting track bioelectric
potential, chlorophyll fluorescence and sap flow, and turn them into sound — the
forest composing its own music, live, on an eleven-year cycle from 2026 to 2036.
Eleven years of canopy, crown, soil carbon and sand movement become a four-voice
score in which noise dominates at the start and resolves into harmony by the end.

## 5 · How to help

**Take a cell.** Sponsorship is not an anonymous donation. Each USD 30 corresponds
to one square metre with a fixed, named location — a specific, verifiable unit of
restoration rather than a line in a ledger. **Ten years of maintenance and
irrigation are included in the price.**

| Tier | Amount | Area | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Entry** | $30 | 1 m² | Name engraved on the cell, NFC card |
| **Supporter** | $300 | 10 m² | NFC card, cell location, growth report |
| **Partner** | $3,000 | 100 m² · 1 cell | Named cell, drone documentation, annual report |

Sponsorship confers stewardship and recognition, not title to land. The seven
infrastructure cells are project infrastructure and are not offered for
sponsorship.

**Larger partners** commit against the parts a per-square-metre price cannot carry
on its own: the laboratory, the Hub building and the certification stack. These are
the conversations to have in person.

**Other ways in**, in rough order of how much they help:

- **Fund a training cohort** — Community's 120 children a year is the number that turns restoration into a reason for a family to stay.
- **Contribute in kind** — drilling, solar, irrigation hardware, logistics and haulage across the border are the costs that move the budget most.
- **Take the data** — the Lab's MRV methodology and satellite baseline are available to researchers and to partners who need carbon accounting and ESG reporting.
- **Carry the story** — the artworks are legible from orbit, which makes them unusually easy to show to people who will never stand on the site.
